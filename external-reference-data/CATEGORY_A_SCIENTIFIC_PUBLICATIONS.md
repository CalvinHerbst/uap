## 1. Robert Hastings — Nuclear-UAP Correlation Dataset

**SOURCE:** *UFOs & Nukes: Extraordinary Encounters at Nuclear Weapons Sites* (2nd Edition, revised/updated)
**TYPE:** Investigative research (interview-based + FOIA-corroborated), book-length study
**URL:** https://www.ufohastings.com/ (author site) | Available via Amazon/Barnes & Noble (ISBN 9781544822198)
**DATE:** Originally published 2008; 2nd edition ~2017; audiobook 2024
**RELEVANCE:** Directly tests Sub-Theory 6 (Nuclear Development Monitor) — the strongest signal in your PURSUE dataset. Provides the most comprehensive external dataset of nuclear-facility UAP incidents for cross-referencing against your 28 nuclear-proximate PURSUE cases and 17-case Oak Ridge cluster.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- 170+ former USAF personnel interviewed over five decades regarding UAP encounters at nuclear weapons sites
- Documented UAP activity at plutonium processing facility in Hanford, Washington State as early as January 1945 — pre-dating Trinity and Hiroshima by months
- Key facility sites documented: Malmstrom AFB (Montana), Minot AFB (North Dakota), Loring AFB (Maine), Wurtsmith AFB (Michigan), F.E. Warren AFB (Wyoming), White Sands, Los Alamos, Kirtland/Sandia, Oak Ridge
- Malmstrom March 1967: 10 Minuteman ICBMs simultaneously went to "No-Go" status while disc-shaped craft hovered over launch facilities (Echo Flight, March 16; Oscar Flight, March 24). NOTE: A 2025 Pentagon report attributed shutdowns to a classified EMP device test; witnesses and the Boeing engineer who inspected missiles dispute this, stating no EMP-consistent damage signature was present
- Minot AFB ~1966-67: Captain David Schur reported luminous disc activating "launch in progress" indicators on 7-8 of 10 missiles sequentially as it moved between silos
- October 1982 Soviet incident (documents from Russia): disc hovered over Ukrainian IRBM base; missiles entered unauthorized countdown; terminated after 15 seconds
- October 2010 F.E. Warren AFB (Wyoming): missile base lost communications with several launch control facilities while cigar-shaped craft flew over them
- Loring AFB (Maine) October 1975: giant cigar-shaped craft over SAC base during Cuban Missile Crisis era; National Military Command Center memos confirm senior-level attention
- Pattern: activity "occasionally transcends mere surveillance and involves direct and unambiguous interference with strategic weapons systems"
- 2010 National Press Club conference (CNN live-streamed): 7 former USAF officers testified publicly about nuclear-UAP incidents

**CROSS-REFERENCE AGAINST PURSUE:**
- Oak Ridge cluster (R01-0357, 0358, 0360, 0401, 0403, 0404, 0406–0410, 0440, 0651): Hastings documents additional Oak Ridge incidents not in FBI files — potential expansion of the 17-case cluster
- White Sands missile-following (R01-0229, 0364): Hastings independently documents White Sands activity
- Geographic migration pattern (Pacific NW → Oak Ridge → White Sands → Kirtland): Hastings' facility list maps precisely onto your quantified nuclear center-of-gravity migration
- Malmstrom/Minot incidents are NOT in PURSUE Release 01 — their absence is itself a data point supporting your "what's missing" analysis

**CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM-HIGH — FOIA documents corroborate many accounts; witness testimony is the primary evidence for most cases. Institutional incentive runs toward credibility (witnesses risked career consequences). However, the Malmstrom case has been specifically challenged: skeptics argue the UFO was Mars and unrelated to the malfunction, and the 2025 Pentagon EMP attribution (if accurate) would remove the strongest single case. The broader pattern across dozens of facilities remains robust even if individual cases are contested.

**LIMITATION:** The full incident list with dates/locations/facility types is not available as a downloadable structured dataset — it must be extracted from the book. No machine-readable cross-reference table exists. The data is interview-based, not instrument-based.

## 2. SCU — Nimitz Carrier Strike Group Analysis (2004 Encounter)

**SOURCE:** "A Forensic Analysis of Navy Carrier Strike Group Eleven's Encounter with an Anomalous Aerial Vehicle"
**TYPE:** Peer-reviewed technical analysis (SCU internal review process)
**URL:** https://www.explorescu.org/post/2004-uss-nimitz-strike-navy-group-incident-report
**DATE:** Published 2019 (investigation began 2016)
**RELEVANCE:** Tests performance envelope consistency signal. Provides quantified acceleration, speed, and power estimates for the most well-documented modern UAP encounter. Cross-references against PURSUE performance data.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- 270-page report combining ATFLIR video analysis, radar data (SPY-1B), pilot testimony, FOIA documents
- Two-week period (November 2004): USS Nimitz CSG-11 encountered up to 100 UAVs
- USS Princeton SPY-1B radar tracked objects descending from near-orbit (~80,000+ ft) to 28,000 ft to sea level
- Calculated descent from 28,000 ft to sea level in 0.78 seconds — approximately 5,880 g acceleration
- ATFLIR video analysis: accelerations from 40g to hundreds of g-forces
- Estimated power requirement: 1-9 gigawatts (assuming 1-ton mass) — exceeds total US nuclear power production
- No sonic boom, no thermal signature commensurate with calculated energy, no visible exhaust
- Object actively jammed radar lock (Lt. Underwood's attempt) — implies knowledge of US military radar frequencies
- Authors: Robert Powell, Peter Reali, Tim Thompson, Morgan Beall, Doug Kimzey, Larry Cates, Richard Hoffman

**COMPANION PEER-REVIEWED PAPER:**
- Knuth, Powell, Reali. "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles." *Entropy* 21, no. 10 (2019): 939. Published in MDPI open-access journal.
- Estimated accelerations: 75g to >5,000g
- Available at PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7514271/
- Conclusion: "These observations are either fabricated or seriously in error, or these craft exhibit technology far more advanced than any known craft on Earth"

**CROSS-REFERENCE AGAINST PURSUE:**
- Nimitz case is NOT in PURSUE Release 01 — a Navy-originated case excluded from a release that contains zero Navy material
- Performance parameters (hypersonic speed, instantaneous acceleration, no sonic boom) match your irreducible anomalous core criteria precisely
- Object behavior (reactive/evasive, radar jamming) matches behavioral profile 3 (reactive/evasive) from your sub-theory analysis

**CONFIDENCE:** HIGH for the analysis methodology; MEDIUM for conclusions (derived from limited publicly available data; classified data may alter findings). The Knuth/Powell/Reali paper is peer-reviewed and published in an indexed journal. The SCU report itself underwent internal peer review but is not journal-published.

## 3. SCU — Aguadilla (Puerto Rico) UAP Analysis (2013 Encounter)

**SOURCE:** "2013 Aguadilla Puerto Rico UAP: The detailed analysis of an Unidentified Anomalous Phenomenon captured by the Department of Homeland Security"
**TYPE:** Technical analysis with radar cross-reference (SCU, v2)
**URL:** https://zenodo.org/records/7844175
**DATE:** Published August 6, 2015 (v2); Zenodo DOI archived
**RELEVANCE:** Tests trans-medium travel claims. One of very few cases with government IR video analyzed frame-by-frame with corroborating radar data. Tests Sub-Theory 4 (Automated Probes) and Sub-Theory 5 (Forward Operating Base — ocean-proximate basing).

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- Video captured by US Customs and Border Protection DHC-8 aircraft, Wescam MX-15D IR camera (3-5 micron MWIR)
- Video authenticity verified via radar data correlated to metadata
- Object demonstrated trans-medium travel: air to water and back
- Object appeared to split into two separate objects near end of clip
- Low-altitude, high-speed flight over coastal environment
- DHS and USAF both declined to investigate when CBP crew attempted internal review
- Authors: Robert Powell, Morgan Beall, Larry Cates, Carl Paulson, Richard Hoffman, Daina Chaviano
- AARO referenced this incident in Senate subcommittee hearing

**CROSS-REFERENCE AGAINST PURSUE:**
- Trans-medium behavior matches PURSUE ocean-surface cases (R01-0024 90° turns over water, R01-0025 surface flight, R01-0020 sphere with water reflection)
- Object splitting matches Western US Event "mother orb" behavior
- Caribbean/maritime location relevant to ocean-basing hypothesis

**CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM-HIGH — authenticated government video with radar corroboration. Frame-by-frame analysis methodology is documented and reproducible. Some skeptical analyses have proposed the object was a lantern or bird; the trans-medium behavior and object-splitting are the most contested claims.

Establishes institutional legitimacy for UAP as scientific subject. ## 5. Blue Book Special Report No. 14 (Battelle Memorial Institute, 1955)

**SOURCE:** "Analysis of Reports of Unidentified Aerial Objects" — Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14
**TYPE:** Government-commissioned statistical analysis (USAF/Battelle)
**URL:** https://archive.org/details/ProjectBlueBookSpecialReport14 (full text, free download) | CIA Reading Room also hosts a complete scan
**DATE:** Dated May 5, 1955 (analysis of cases through 1954)
**RELEVANCE:** Tests all sub-theories — the largest systematic statistical study of UAP cases ever conducted. The inverse quality-unknown correlation is the single most important statistical finding in UAP history and directly relevant to evaluating your PURSUE data quality flags.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- 3,201 cases analyzed by Battelle scientists using ~30 encoded report characteristics on IBM punch cards
- Four analysts classified each case; "Unknown" required unanimous agreement of all four (stringent criterion); "Known" required only two
- Results: ~69% Known, ~22% Unknown (693 cases), ~9% Insufficient Information
- **Critical finding:** Unknown rate INCREASED with case quality — 35% of "Excellent" quality cases were Unknowns vs. 18% of "Poor" quality cases. This directly contradicts the claim that only bad reports remain unexplained
- 100% of "Unknown" determinations were rated "certain" by analysts, vs. only ~50% "certain" for solved cases
- Six characteristics compared between Knowns and Unknowns (color, number, duration, brightness, shape, speed) — statistically significant differences found
- Despite these findings, the report's summary declared it "highly improbable that any of the reports represent observations of extraterrestrial vehicles" — a famous disconnect between data and conclusions
- French GEPAN/SEPRA (1977-2004) independently replicated this pattern: ~13% defied explanation across 6,000 scientifically investigated cases
- The NICAP structured list of all 701 Blue Book Unknowns (with case numbers, dates, locations) is available at https://www.nicap.org/bluebook/bluelist.htm

**CROSS-REFERENCE AGAINST PURSUE:**
- The Blue Book era (1947-1969) overlaps directly with 620 of your 721 PURSUE incidents
- Your data_quality flags (digital vs. OCR) can be used as a rough proxy for case quality — test whether the SR-14 inverse correlation holds in the PURSUE dataset
- The 701 Blue Book Unknowns list is directly matchable against your PURSUE incident IDs by date and location
- SR-14's statistical methodology provides a template for analyzing your own 721-case dataset

**CONFIDENCE:** HIGH — primary government record, fully declassified, reproducible methodology. The data itself is robust; the editorial conclusions are widely regarded as institutionally motivated and disconnected from the statistical findings.

## 6. Galileo Project — Commissioning Data & IM1 Spherules

### 6a. Galileo Project Observatory Commissioning Data

**SOURCE:** "Commissioning Data on Half a Million Objects in the Sky from the Galileo Project Observatory: Are Any of Them UAP?"
**TYPE:** Scientific preprint (under peer review as of late 2024)
**URL:** Paper linked from https://avi-loeb.medium.com (November 12, 2024 post)
**DATE:** November 2024 (data collected January-May 2024)
**RELEVANCE:** Tests sensor artifact hypothesis. First purpose-built multi-sensor observatory designed specifically for UAP detection. Methodology benchmark for what calibrated, continuous monitoring can achieve.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- 3.5 years from inception to first results (inaugurated July 26, 2021)
- First observatory on roof of Harvard College Observatory; two additional sites in progress (including one atop the Las Vegas Sphere, announced October 2025)
- "Dalek" sensor array: 8 uncooled IR cameras on half-sphere, continuous all-sky monitoring
- Supplementary sensors: optical, radio, magnetic, audio — continuous recording
- ~500,000 objects observed in 5-month commissioning period
- ML algorithms classify objects; most identified as birds, aircraft, satellites, clouds
- Key limitation: single observatory cannot determine object distance (no stereoscopic baseline); planned multi-site deployment will enable triangulation
- Lead author: Dr. Laura Domine (GP postdoc); PI: Avi Loeb
- As of March 2026: Loeb stated GP is "now capable of discovering UAP"

**CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM — methodology is sound and instrument design is novel, but commissioning data has not yet been peer-reviewed. No confirmed anomalous detections reported from the commissioning dataset. The project's value is primarily methodological at this stage.

### 6b. IM1 Interstellar Meteor Spherules

**SOURCE:** "Chemical classification of spherules recovered from the Pacific Ocean site of the CNEOS 2014-01-08 (IM1) bolide"
**TYPE:** Peer-reviewed (published in *Chemical Geology*, Elsevier)
**URL:** https://galileo.hsites.harvard.edu/news/new-paper-chemical-classification-im1-spherules-published-chemical-geology
**DATE:** September 23, 2024 (Chemical Geology publication)
**RELEVANCE:** Tests Sub-Theory 5 (Forward Operating Base) — if confirmed interstellar, establishes that physical material from outside the solar system has reached Earth. Relevant to the question of whether non-human artifacts could persist near Earth.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- CNEOS 2014-01-08: bolide detected January 8, 2014, 84 km north of Manus Island (Papua New Guinea)
- US Space Command issued formal letter certifying 99.999% likelihood of interstellar origin based on velocity (>45 km/s relative to Earth)
- Expedition recovered metallic spherules (<1mm diameter) from ocean floor at calculated impact site
- "BeLaU" spherule subset shows enrichments in Beryllium, Lanthanum, and Uranium up to 1,000x greater than known solar system materials
- Loeb claims compositions suggest "origins that are not yet understood and are unlike any known planetary bodies"

**CRITICAL COUNTEREVIDENCE — FLAG:**
- Fernando et al. (Johns Hopkins/Columbia, March 2024, published in *Geophysical Journal International*): seismic analysis found hundreds of similar tremors in the weeks surrounding the event; concluded recovered spherules are "unrelated to the meteoroid regardless of whether the parent meteoroid was interstellar or Solar system bound"
- Brown & Borovička (2023): CNEOS velocity measurements have significant uncertainties (up to ~8 km/s); the bolide was likely in a bound heliocentric orbit
- Desch & Jackson (2024 preprint, arxiv:2403.05161): Iron isotope ratios of BeLaU spherules are consistent with terrestrial laterites (Earth-origin material); compound spherule morphology inconsistent with ablation origin; conclude spherules are terrestrial contamination
- Independent analyses suggest spherules may be industrial pollution (coal ash has similar composition)

**CONFIDENCE:** LOW for the interstellar/anomalous composition claims — heavily disputed by multiple independent research groups. HIGH for the expedition methodology and the Chemical Geology publication itself. The scientific consensus as of 2026 leans toward terrestrial origin for the spherules, even if the original bolide may have been interstellar.

## 7. Condon Report — Unexplained Case Data (1968)

**SOURCE:** "Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects" (University of Colorado)
**TYPE:** Government-commissioned academic study (USAF-funded)
**URL:** Available at Internet Archive (full text, free download)
**DATE:** 1968 (published January 1969)
**RELEVANCE:** Cross-reference against PURSUE dataset (overlapping cases 1966-1968). Tests all hypotheses — ~30% of examined cases remained unexplained despite investigation. The disconnect between case data and editorial conclusions is itself evidence of institutional behavior (your Signal 5).

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- ~90 cases examined in detail by University of Colorado scientists
- ~30% remained unexplained after investigation
- Edward Condon's conclusions ("further extensive study of UFOs probably cannot be justified") contradicted the case data in the body of the report — several contributing scientists publicly dissented
- Notable unexplained cases within the study:
  - McMinnville photos (1950): Trent photos analyzed; deemed genuine by case investigator William Hartmann
  - RB-47 radar case (1957): electromagnetic sensor, airborne radar, and ground radar simultaneously tracked object for 700 miles
  - Lakenheath-Bentwaters radar-visual (1956): multiple radar returns correlated with visual observations by RAF and USAF personnel
- The report's own case investigator (Roy Craig) acknowledged many strong unknowns
- Directly led to closure of Project Blue Book (December 1969)
- The Condon Committee's internal dysfunction (the "Low memo" scandal revealing pre-determined conclusions) is well-documented

**CROSS-REFERENCE AGAINST PURSUE:**
- The 1966 wave in your PURSUE data (53 incidents, Wave 5) is contemporaneous with the Condon investigation
- Lakenheath-Bentwaters (1956) appears to connect to the "48th Fighter Wing" reference in your PURSUE AARO files — potential 64-year single-location thread
- The institutional behavior pattern (collect data, dismiss publicly) maps directly onto your Signal 5 analysis

**CONFIDENCE:** HIGH for the case data; LOW for the editorial conclusions. The Condon Report is a primary source for understanding institutional behavior toward UAP — both what the data showed and how it was framed.

## 8. Peer-Reviewed Physics Papers Relevant to UAP Propulsion

### 8a. Knuth, Powell, Reali — Flight Characteristics (2019)

**SOURCE:** "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles"
**TYPE:** Peer-reviewed journal article (MDPI Entropy)
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7514271/ (open access)
**DATE:** Published October 2019 (*Entropy* 21, no. 10: 939)
**RELEVANCE:** Directly tests performance envelope consistency. Quantifies the "beyond known technology" claim with physics-based calculations.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**
- Nimitz 2004: accelerations estimated at 75g to >5,000g
- No observed air disturbance, no sonic booms, no thermal signature commensurate with calculated energies
- Power requirement: peaks exceeding total US nuclear power production by factor of 10+
- Authors present three logical options: "(1) fabricated, (2) seriously in error, or (3) technology far more advanced than any known craft on Earth"
- Authors from University at Albany (SUNY) Physics Dept. and SCU
- Knuth is editor-in-chief of *Entropy* — potential conflict of interest noted by critics; however, the paper went through standard peer review

**CONFIDENCE:** HIGH for methodology (Bayesian inference, well-documented calculations). The paper's conclusions depend on the accuracy of witness testimony and the limited publicly available sensor data. If classified data contradicts the witness accounts, the acceleration estimates would change.

### 8b. Szydagis, Knuth, Kugielsky, Levy — UAPx Field Expedition (2025)

**SOURCE:** "Initial results from the first field expedition of UAPx to study unidentified anomalous phenomena"
**TYPE:** Peer-reviewed journal article
**URL:** Published in *Progress in Aerospace Sciences*, Vol. 156, June 2025
**DATE:** June 2025
**RELEVANCE:** Establishes reproducible scientific methodology for UAP field research. Tests sensor artifact hypothesis — demonstrates what calibrated multi-sensor monitoring can and cannot detect.

**KEY DATA POINTS:**

- Published alongside Knuth et al. survey paper in same journal issue

## WHAT'S STILL NEEDED

Several Category A sources were searched but could not be located as freely accessible structured data:

1. **Hastings' full incident list** — The most critical gap. The book documents 170+ witness accounts across dozens of facilities, but no publicly available machine-readable index exists. Extracting this from the book text would be the highest-yield manual research task.

2. **Vallee materials analysis** — Jacques Vallée's published results on isotopic ratios and anomalous material compositions were referenced in interviews but no freely accessible peer-reviewed publication was located with specific analytical data. His Trinity crash analysis (2021) is behind a paywall. This moves to Category C (Physical Evidence).

3. **MHD propulsion papers** — General MHD propulsion theory is well-established in aerospace engineering literature, but no peer-reviewed paper specifically connecting MHD to observed UAP performance characteristics was found. Pais (Navy patents) are patent filings, not peer-reviewed research. The theoretical physics of UAP propulsion remains speculative and unpublished in mainstream journals.

4. **SCU nuclear-site pattern analysis** — Referenced in UAPedia and SCU materials as existing on Zenodo, but the specific structured nuclear-site analysis paper was not located. Worth a direct search on Zenodo and SCU's publications page.
