## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

These three documents are the **foundational incident catalog of the U.S. Air Force's first systematic UFO investigation**, compiled at Wright-Patterson AFB by the Air Materiel Command intelligence directorate. They predate Project Blue Book and appear to be the working files of Project Sign (established January 1948) with some cases dating to the pre-Sign period. Each incident uses a standardized "CHECK-LIST — UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS" form with 26 fields.

This is, historically, the most significant batch of documents in PURSUE Release 01 — it represents the U.S. military's original attempt to systematically catalog and evaluate the UFO phenomenon at the moment it first became a national security concern.

## ANOMALOUS STANDOUTS — HISTORICAL CASES

### Tier 1 — High Credibility, Anomalous Behavior

**1. Muroc AAF (Edwards) Cluster — 8 July 1947 (Incidents 1, 2, 3, 4)**
Four separate observations at the premier US flight test facility on the same day, by military personnel including a **test pilot (Maj. J.C. Wise)** and a **flight test captain (Capt. John Paul Stapp — later famous for rocket sled experiments)**. Objects exhibited oscillating flight, tight circling beyond known aircraft capability, flight against prevailing wind, and metallic construction. Stapp concluded: "I think it was a man-made object, as evidenced by the outline and functional appearance."

**2. Andrews AFB Pursuit — 18 November 1948 (Incidents 207, 207A)**
Two separate Air Force Reserve pilots independently pursued an unidentified object over Andrews AFB on the same night. Lt. Combs closed to 300–400 ft, illuminated the object with his landing light (revealing a dull gray oblong shape with no wings or tail), and documented **airspeed variations from 80 to 500–600 mph**, tight circles inside his own turning radius with flaps down, and vertical climbs. Corroborated by multiple witnesses. Object departed NE at 500–600 mph. This is the most detailed pursuit account in the historical files.

**3. Portland Mass Sighting — 4 July 1947 (Incidents 5–9, 12)**
At least six independent observers including **four police officers (two also private pilots)**, a harbor pilot, and a sheriff's deputy reported disc-shaped objects over Portland, OR at 1305 local time. Objects traveled in formations of 3–5, at "terrific speed," with one performing 90° turns. A sheriff's deputy across the river in Vancouver reported 20–30 objects in "V" formation with a "low buzzing sound." Multiple corroborated accounts.

**4. January 7, 1948 Ohio Cluster (Incidents 30, 32, 33 series, 48 series)**
A massive multi-location event across Ohio on the same evening as the Mantell incident in Kentucky. Control tower operators at Clinton County AAF observed an object for 35 minutes that exhibited: hovering, terrific speed bursts, vertical ascents/descents, shape changes (triangle ↔ cone), color changes (white → red → green), green exhaust trail, and visibility through cloud cover. Six military personnel witnessed. Cpl. Hudson provided four penciled sketches.

**5. Incident 134 — Selfridge Field, 28 May 1948**
Lt. Kokolonis and M/Sgt Davis observed five disc-shaped objects estimated at 300–500 ft in length, traveling at 500–1,000+ mph near Monroe, Michigan. Objects maintained nose-up attitude, made abrupt turns, and had a "hazy or fuzzy outline." The size estimate (larger than lake freighters) is among the largest in the historical files.

### Tier 2 — Notable

**6. Kenneth Arnold — 24 June 1947 (Incident 17)**
The foundational sighting. Nine circular mirror-like objects near Mt. Rainier at 9,500 ft traveling N→S. Arnold's speed estimate (calculated from time between two known peaks) yielded ~1,200 mph, which was beyond any known aircraft in 1947. Form notes the objects appeared to "whirl or spin" and travel in "sidewise position."

**7. Incident 152 — Indianapolis Cymbal, 31 July 1948**
Mr. & Mrs. Swigert observed a cymbal-shaped object (20 ft diameter, 3:1 ratio) at 2,000 ft traveling east at an estimated 1,890 mph. Object shimmered as if spinning. Investigated by base intelligence officer. No other reports received.

**8. Incident 233 — Jackson, MS, 1 January 1949**
AAF veteran pilot Tom Rush and his private-pilot wife observed a cigar-shaped object at 1,500 ft that crossed 500 ft in front of their Stinson, made a 90° turn (at which point Rush noticed it had no wings), and accelerated from 200 to 400–600 mph. Investigation of adjacent airfields "could throw no light on the incident."

**9. Ghost Rocket Context — Incident 150 (Norway/Sweden, ~1 Mar 1948)**
Norwegian military pilot observed a projectile at 20,000 ft along the Swedish coast, disappearing in 4 seconds. The file notes: "Similar missiles have been observed at Rjuken, Norway & Finse, Norway. It is of interest to know that heavy water is currently being produced in Rjuken." This connects the files to the European "ghost rocket" phenomenon.

## PROCEDURAL & INSTITUTIONAL OBSERVATIONS

**Evaluation methodology:** The analysts used explicit credibility assessments. "Confirmed by other sources," "Corroborated report," "Corroborated account" appear on the highest-confidence cases. Negative assessments include: "individual appears to be crackpot," "Sgt Wright's character and honesty are questionable," and "no further investigation — only one person witnessed."

**Cross-referencing system:** The files extensively cross-reference related incidents (e.g., "See Incidents 48, 48a, 48b, 48c, 48d & 33, 33a, 33d, 33c, 33d, 33e, 33f and 30 and 32"), indicating systematic analysis of event clusters rather than isolated case treatment.

**Meteor/astronomical filtering:** Multiple incidents (101, 223, 223a, 224, 227, 230) are explicitly analyzed against known meteor showers (Perseids, December meteors). Incident 101 was definitively resolved when 109 lbs of achondrite meteorite were recovered. This shows active conventional-explanation filtering.

**International intelligence pipeline:** Finnish sightings (99, 100) came via USMILATTACHE Helsinki; Norwegian/Swedish observations via military intelligence channels. The Norwegian ghost rocket reference (Incident 150) connects to the heavy water production site at Rjuken, suggesting Cold War context was being actively considered.

**White Sands awareness:** Incident 231 prompted inquiry to Holloman AF Base, which "revealed that research work at night and on Sundays does not involve the use of any lighting device or flares such as described." This shows systematic cross-checking against military activity.

## WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE PURSUE CATALOG

These 233 incidents fill the most critical gap in the Phase 1 catalog: the 1947–1949 period that launched the entire US government UFO investigation. Combined with the modern DOW/AARO material, we now have bookends:

- **1947–1949:** The phenomenon as initially encountered — disc-shaped, metallic, oscillating, performing maneuvers beyond known capability, concentrated in the Pacific NW and near military test facilities (Muroc/Edwards, Wright-Patterson)
- **2016–2025:** The phenomenon as currently documented — spherical/orb-shaped, often IR-only, concentrated in USCENTCOM theater, formally integrated into military reporting

The morphological shift from disc → sphere and the geographic shift from domestic US → overseas theater are the two most striking evolutionary patterns across the 78-year span of this dataset.
